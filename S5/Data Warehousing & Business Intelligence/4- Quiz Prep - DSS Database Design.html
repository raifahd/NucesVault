Quiz Prep: DSS Database Design

1 Database Design & Normalization

Test your knowledge on 1NF, 2NF, and 3NF

Practice Questions
Easy: What is the main requirement of the First Normal Form (1NF)?

Answer: The domain of each attribute must include only atomic (simple, indivisible) values. There should be no repeating groups or multiple domains combined into a single attribute.

Medium: An insurance table stores policy status. A status code "L" means "lapsed" for Long-Term Disability policies, but means "free look" for Life policies. Which normal form does this violate and why?

Answer: It violates 1NF because the value's meaning is "redefined" based on another attribute's domain. The attribute is overloaded, meaning it is not atomic in its semantics.

Medium: What is the rule for Second Normal Form (2NF)?

Answer: Every non-prime attribute must be fully functionally dependent on the entire primary key, not just a part of a composite primary key.

Hard: A shipment table has the primary key `Shipment#`. It also contains `Customer#`, `Customer_Nm`, and `Address`. Why does this violate 3NF, and what is the fix?

Answer: It violates 3NF because `Customer_Nm` and `Address` are transitively dependent on the primary key `Shipment#` through the non-prime attribute `Customer#`. The fix is to split the table into fundamental entities: a `Shipment` table and a separate `Customer` table.

2 Denormalization Techniques

Pre-joins, Column Replication, and Pre-aggregation

Practice Questions
Easy: What are the three common forms of denormalization used in DSS databases?

Answer: 1) Pre-join denormalization, 2) Column replication or movement, and 3) Pre-aggregation.

Medium: When applying pre-join denormalization between a Sales Header and Sales Detail table, what happens to the overall raw data size?

Answer: The storage requirements typically increase significantly because the header information (e.g., store ID, date) is redundantly replicated for every single line item in the detail table.

Medium: Why might a database designer choose to replicate a `customer_id` from a header table down into a deeply nested transaction detail table?

Answer: To avoid a costly multi-table join and to facilitate table co-location, ensuring that all transactions for a given customer are stored on the same disk partitions, which drastically speeds up customer-centric queries.

Hard: Why is it stated that "Aggregates should never replace detailed data" in a Data Warehouse?

Answer: While pre-aggregation provides huge performance gains for standard, pre-defined queries, ad-hoc discovery and sophisticated drill-down analyses will always require the raw, detailed data. Deleting the details removes the flexibility of the DSS.

Hard: What are the primary tradeoffs that must be quantified when considering any denormalization technique?

Answer: Every denormalization decision is an engineering tradeoff between: 1) Query Performance (usually the benefit), against 2) Storage Cost, 3) Flexibility (ability to answer new types of queries), and 4) Maintenance (ETL refresh costs and data quality management).
