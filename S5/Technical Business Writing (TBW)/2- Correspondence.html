1Business Letters

Formatting, structures, and types of external business correspondence

Business letters are primarily written to inform readers of specific information, persuade others to take action, or act as advertisements (e.g., sales letters). They are usually reserved for external communication.

Letter Format

The fully block open punctuation style is the most common format. All elements are lined up along an invisible line down the left side of the page. Text is single-spaced, with no indentation for the first sentence of paragraphs.

Parts of a Business Letter

  • Letterhead / Heading: Conveys a positive image (logo, address, phone). If no letterhead is available, place your full return address and date.
  • Date: Avoid abbreviating the month.
  • Inside Address: The recipient’s full name, title/designation, and address.
  • Subject Line: Optional element below the inside address to focus the topic (especially useful if addressing a large company).
  • Salutation: Use title + last name + colon (e.g., Dear Ms. Asma:). Avoid "To Whom It May Concern".
  • Body: Starts one line below salutation. Contains: Purpose, Details, Call for Action, and Closing off.
  • Complimentary Closing: Sincerely, or Best regards, (Capitalize only the first letter).
  • Writer's Signature Block: Typed name 4 lines below the closing, with the handwritten signature in the gap.
  • End Notations: Reference initials, Enclosures, or Copy notations (cc/bcc).

Common Types of Business Letters

Letter Type Purpose / Definition
1. Letter of Acknowledgement Sent to acknowledge receipt of something when a written record is appropriate. May state that the matter will be looked into further.
2. Letter of Enquiry Requesting information about goods/services. Must be brief and clear (catalogue, price list, quotation).
3. Letter of Complaint Stating facts about defective goods/services and asking for a resolution. Must avoid rudeness at all costs.
4. Adjustment Letter The response to a complaint letter. Used to investigate, explain, apologize, and put things right.
General Guidelines
1. Know your purpose and readers • 2. Follow ABC Format (Abstract, Body, Conclusion) • 3. Use the "You" Attitude • 4. Be diplomatic • 5. Avoid technical jargon for external readers.

2Memorandums (Memos)

Internal correspondence, documentation, and procedures

A memo is used internally within an organization (moving upward, downward, or horizontally). It is never sent outside the company.

Memo vs. Letter
Letter: External communication, requires inside address, salutation, complimentary close, avoids jargons.
Memo: Internal communication, omits salutation/close, relies on TO / FROM / DATE / SUBJECT, and can use company-specific technical jargon safely.

Purposes of Memos

  • Documentation: Reporting expenses, incidents, accidents, problems, hiring, or costs.
  • Procedures: Explaining how to set up accounts, operate machinery, or use software.
  • Cover/Transmittal: Telling the reader you have attached a document.
  • Confirmation: Documenting meeting agendas, decisions to purchase, or conclusions arrived at.
  • Feasibility: Studying the possibility of changes (e.g., relocating, buying new equipment).
  • Status/Progress: Providing weekly/monthly progress reports on sales, staffing, etc.

Key Components of a Memo

Memo Heading Format
DATE:    October 21, 2026
TO:      Naveed Ahmed, Manager Product Development
FROM:    Asad Irfan, Customer Support
SUBJECT: Salary Increase For Comptrollers

The rest of the memo follows the ABC Format:

  • Introduction (Abstract): A lead-in/overview stating why you are writing and what it's about.
  • Discussion (Body): Detailed development made accessible through highlighting techniques (lists/bullets).
  • Conclusion: A summation stating what is next, when it will occur, and why the date is important (e.g., A directive close or complimentary close).
Flawed Subject Lines
One-word subject lines like Subject: COMPTROLLERS don't communicate effectively. Always use a descriptive focus + topic: Subject: Salary Increase For Comptrollers.

3Email Writing & Guidelines

Maintaining professionalism in electronic correspondence

Emails have replaced most routine memos and casual letters, but they still require a high level of professionalism.

Essential Email Hygiene

  • Professionalism: Do not forward jokes/spam, discuss gossip, or use biased language.
  • No Flames: Never send emails with abusive, obscene, or derogatory language to attack someone.
  • Username: Use professional usernames (e.g., msmith@domain.com), not clever or hobby-related ones.
  • Emoticons & Abbreviations: Do not use smileys or casual chat text abbreviations (e.g., BTW, LOL).
  • Subject Line: Must describe the topic and focus. When forwarding, revise the subject line to reflect current content.
  • Copying (CC): Use the courtesy copy thoughtfully to keep others informed without cluttering inboxes.
  • Attachments: Always include a cover message inside the email body explaining the attachment ("Attached is a copy of my budget proposal...").
  • Formatting: Do not write in ALL UPPERCASE (shouting) or all lowercase. Ensure no grammatical typos.

4Correspondence Case Studies

Practice writing scenarios from past papers

Case Study Questions
Writing a Complaint Letter (Software Error)

Scenario: You are the CEO of Avanceon (Lahore). You outsourced a software project to Social Square. After a month, it faced an error. Write a complaint letter to Manager Customer Support (Mr. Adil Rizwan). Identify that you CC'd the CEO of Social Square and enclosed a screenshot.

Answer Outline:

  • Heading: Avanceon Letterhead + Date + Inside Address (Adil Rizwan, Social Square).
  • Subject: Complaint Regarding Software Error in Salary Management Program.
  • Salutation: Dear Mr. Rizwan:
  • Introduction: State the purchase/installation details and the exact problem clearly but politely.
  • Body: Explain the impact of the error. Reference the enclosed screenshot.
  • Conclusion/Action: Request immediate troubleshooting/fixing according to the contract terms.
  • Closing: Sincerely, [Your Name/CEO].
  • End Notations: Enclosure: Error Screenshot. cc: Mr. Avais, CEO Social Square.
Writing an Adjustment Letter

Scenario: You are the Customer Support Manager at Social Square. Write an adjustment letter to the CEO of Avanceon to address the complaint above, keeping them as a long-term customer.

Answer Outline:

  • Subject: Resolution of Software Error in Salary Management Program.
  • Opening: Thank them for bringing it to your attention and apologize for the inconvenience ("Please accept my apologies for...").
  • Body (Explanation): Explain that your team investigated and found the root cause (e.g., an unauthorized IP access attempt). Clarify what is being done to resolve it immediately.
  • Action/Resolution: State the timeline for the fix and offer assurance of continued support.
  • Closing: We value Avanceon as a long-term partner and hope you won't face further problems.
Writing a Memo (New Timings Policy)

Scenario: As Administrative Manager at Inspire Limited, write a memo to all employees regarding a new policy enforcing 9-5 timings, due to recent late arrivals and early departures reducing productivity. Outline penalties for non-compliance.

Answer Outline:

DATE:    October 15, 2026
TO:      All Employees, Inspire Limited
FROM:    [Your Name], Administrative Manager
SUBJECT: Implementation of Strict Office Timings Policy

[Introduction]
This memo is to formally address the recent trend of irregular office attendance and outline the new timings policy effective immediately. 

[Discussion/Body]
A review of last month's entry/exit logs showed significant delays in arrival and early departures. This inconsistency severely affects our team's daily productivity, delays project timelines, and increases the workload on punctual staff members.

To ensure fairness and efficiency, the company is enforcing the standard 9:00 AM to 5:00 PM working hours. All employees are required to be at their desks by 9:00 AM. 

[Penalty/Conclusion]
Employees who arrive late or leave early more than twice a month without prior approval will face a deduction in their monthly leave quota, followed by formal disciplinary action. We appreciate your cooperation in maintaining a professional work environment.
